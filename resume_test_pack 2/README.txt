DETAILED J.B. HUNT RECRUITING TEST PACK

10 fictional PDF resumes intended for technology-event and recruiter-card testing. Most profiles target software, data, QA, or UX opportunities; one targets transportation operations. Each includes education, skills, dated experience, detailed project contributions, accomplishments, and availability.

All candidates, employment histories, projects, awards, and metrics are synthetic. Employer names are invented. Real university names are used only as fictional educational context. These are not real applicants, J.B. Hunt-approved resumes, or claims of actual J.B. Hunt job requirements. No candidate is portrayed as a J.B. Hunt employee.

Role mix was informed by J.B. Hunt public recruiting pages, accessed October 7, 2026:
https://careers.jbhunt.com/careers/internships
https://careers.jbhunt.com/careers/technology
The particular stacks and resume achievements are illustrative, not requirements from a specific vacancy.

Files are selectable-text PDFs. See test_cases.csv for candidate roles, page counts, and edge cases. Riley Chen intentionally has no email or phone. Sam Taylor has a certificate and transferable work history but no GPA. Casey Morgan has a stated career break. Morgan Davis has a two-page senior profile.

Suggested checks:
1. Upload each file and verify name, school, degree, graduation date, skills, and contact extraction.
2. Confirm missing contacts and GPA remain missing rather than being invented.
3. Check long names/titles and dense skill lists on desktop and mobile cards.
4. Verify highlight descriptions retain project context and distinguish prototype, simulated, and production outcomes.
5. Ensure in-progress certifications are not labeled completed.
6. Confirm the parser reads all pages and replacing a resume clears stale highlights.

PDFs may contain one or two pages depending on profile density; all carry a discreet fictional-data footer.
